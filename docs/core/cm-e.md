CM-E is the fifth and last core movement in the Saber Standard.

It is a quick movement that combines multiple partner attacks, and it works as either a 1-on-1 or a 2-on-1 scenario. Special moves act as milestones between switching partners.

## Notation

CM-E is written in two tables — one for each half of the exchange.

**First exchange**

| Player | s1 | s2 | s3 | s4 | s5 | s6 | s7 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Saberist A | 9 | 4 | 8 | 5 | 11 | 3X | Special |
| Saberist B | 9P | 4P | 8P | 5P | 11P | 3X | |

**Second exchange**

| Player | s1 | s2 | s3 | s4 | s5 | s6 | s7 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Saberist A | 2P | 10P | 3P | 7P | 1X | Special | |
| Saberist B or C | 2 | 10 | 3 | 7 | 1X | | |

Three things to read carefully:

- **The roles invert between tables.** Saberist A attacks through the first exchange and defends through the second.
- **`X` is a lock.** It appears in both rows at the same step, because a lock is something the two Saberists do together rather than one doing to the other.
- **The third row label is a choice.** `Saberist B or C` is what makes CM-E a 2-on-1 movement: the second exchange may be answered by the same partner or by a new one stepping in.

## Telegraph

**Saber goes horizontally across the chest in a slow cut.**

The telegraph is the silent signal that calls CM-E before an exchange begins. See [LUMINA Games](../games/index.md) for how telegraphs are used in play.

## Special moves and counters

CM-E introduces the **special move**: a step written into the notation as `Special` rather than as a target line.

Each exchange runs a short series of attacks, closes into a lock, and resolves with a special move. The special move is a milestone — it marks the seam where partners switch, and it is where a movement stops being a drill and starts being a performance.

Because the notation names the slot without prescribing its contents, CM-E is the first movement that leaves a decision to the Saberists performing it. What fills a `Special` is chosen for the scene being built; what the notation guarantees is that both Saberists know a special move belongs there, and when.

The lock at `3X` and `1X` is what sets it up. A lock brings the exchange to a standstill and gives both Saberists a shared, stationary moment — the natural place to change partners or resolve.

## Purpose

CM-E is designed to teach:

- combining attacks from multiple partners in one movement
- 1-on-1 and 2-on-1 scenarios from the same notation
- locks, noted `X`
- the `Special` slot and where it belongs in a sequence
- switching partners at a written milestone
- inverting attacker and defender between exchanges

## Training method

### Phase 1: Static walkthrough

Run each exchange separately, and run it 1-on-1 before attempting 2-on-1.

Take the attacks one at a time, then set the lock deliberately. After each step, confirm:

- both Saberists know who is attacking in this exchange
- the lock is entered with control, not collided into
- distance is safe
- the movement can be repeated

Decide what the special move will be before running the sequence. Do not improvise it during practice.

### Phase 2: Slow continuous flow

Perform both exchanges without stopping, still 1-on-1.

The seam is the lock into the special move. Practice steps 5 through 7 of the first exchange on their own until the transition out of the lock is smooth.

If either Saberist loses the sequence, stop and restart from Step 1.

### Phase 3: Add the second opponent

Only once the 1-on-1 version is clean, bring in Saberist C for the second exchange.

The incoming Saberist must know the whole sequence, not just their own entry. Walk the switch statically before running it.

## Teaching note

CM-E should be taught as a language drill, not as a fight.

Two hazards belong to this movement specifically. A lock puts two Saberists at close quarters with blades engaged, so it is entered slowly and by agreement. And a 2-on-1 exchange means someone is moving into the space while an exchange is already live — that entry is choreographed, never spontaneous.

CM-E completes the Core Library. When it is clean, both Saberists have the shared vocabulary that every later movement builds on, and the [Extended Library](extended-library.md) is open.
